# Biomedical Instrumentation — Key Concepts

**Subject:** Biomedical Instrumentation  
**Department:** Biomedical Engineering, Easwari Engineering College

---

## 📌 What is Biomedical Instrumentation?
The application of engineering principles to design instruments 
that measure, monitor, and record physiological signals from the human body.

---

## 🔬 Generalised Instrumentation System
Every biomedical instrument has these stages:

1. **Transducer/Sensor** — converts physiological signal to electrical signal
2. **Signal Conditioner** — amplifies, filters, and processes the signal
3. **Display/Recording** — shows or stores the output (monitor, printer, computer)

---

## ⚡ Important Sensors and Transducers

| Sensor Type | Measures | Example |
|-------------|----------|---------|
| ECG Electrodes | Electrical activity of heart | Ag/AgCl electrodes |
| Pressure Transducer | Blood pressure | Piezoelectric sensor |
| Pulse Oximeter | SpO2 (oxygen saturation) | Photodiode + LED |
| Thermistor | Body temperature | NTC thermistor |
| EMG Electrodes | Muscle electrical activity | Surface electrodes |

---

## 🫀 ECG — Electrocardiogram
- Records electrical activity of the heart
- Standard 12-lead ECG system
- Key waves: P wave, QRS complex, T wave
- P wave = atrial depolarisation
- QRS = ventricular depolarisation
- T wave = ventricular repolarisation

---

## 📊 Key Specifications of Biomedical Instruments
- **Sensitivity** — smallest change the instrument can detect
- **Accuracy** — closeness to true value
- **Resolution** — smallest increment measurable
- **Bandwidth** — frequency range the instrument can handle
- **Noise** — unwanted signal interference

---

## 📌 Summary
Biomedical instruments convert biological signals → electrical signals → 
processed → displayed. Every instrument must be safe, accurate, and reliable 
for clinical use.
